# Reasoning effort 75 (`high`) vs 100 (`max`) on a harder set (2026-09-24)

Follow-up to [effort-and-retention-validation-20260923.md](effort-and-retention-validation-20260923.md)
§1. That set scored 100% at both 50 and 75, so it could bound cost but not quality.
This run asks the question the repl proxy's policy depends on (OPP
`v41-effort-passthrough-and-policy` part (2)): may teacher/review traffic drop from `max`
(100) to `high` (75) with no quality loss?

## Setup

- **Stack.** The live 2×GB10 stack of the 09-23 doc (EXL3 2.9 bpw, DSpark k=3,
  `MAX_NUM_SEQS=2`, MNBT 1024), direct to `192.168.1.97:8000`, not through the proxy.
- **Harness.** `scripts/effort_ab.py --set hard --efforts 75,100 --reps 2 --concurrency 2
  --max-tokens 32768`.
  - The set is `scripts/effort_items_hard.py`: 15 competition-style math items with
    changed numbers (answers computed in-script, exact integer match on the final
    `ANSWER:` line) and 6 coding tasks, each checked against a brute-force reference on
    seeded random inputs, run network-less.
  - Before the run, every correct solution passed and 8 wrong ones failed.
- **Sampling and pairing.** Thinking on, official sampling (temperature 1.0, top_p 0.95).
  Integer efforts go in `chat_template_kwargs`; `/tokenize` confirms 100 ≡ `max` and
  75 ≡ `high`. Arms are interleaved per item, so load hits both equally.
- **Size.** 84 requests = 21 items × 2 efforts × 2 reps. 15:43–17:34 CEST, no errors, no
  length cuts.
- **Summary.** `scripts/effort_ab_paired.py` (new): the paired per-item summary.
- **Raw data.** `_repl_measurement_stash/effort-ab-hard-20260924/`: `rows.jsonl`,
  `run.log`, `paired.txt`.

## Result

| effort | math | code | all | mean tok | median tok | p90 tok | median s | tok/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 75 | 28/30 | 12/12 | **40/42** (95.2%) | 4,903 | 2,684 | 12,649 | 72.0 | 36.8 |
| 100 | 30/30 | 12/12 | **42/42** (100%) | 6,414 | 3,657 | 18,878 | 100.0 | 36.3 |

- **Quality.** Two discordant pairs, and both favour 100: m9 rep 0 (lattice points in
  x²+y² ≤ 2026) and m12 rep 1 (7-digit palindromes divisible by 11). They were right at
  100 and wrong at 75. No pair went the other way.
  - Each miss is the shortest trace for its item: m9 used 3,173 tokens against 7,449 for
    its correct 75 rep; m12 used 2,064 against 4,771. That is consistent with too little
    thinking.
  - Both ended `stop` with content. The harness does not store the reply, so a wrong
    number cannot be told from a slip in the `ANSWER:` line.
  - Exact McNemar on 2 vs 0 gives p = 0.5, so the difference is **not significant**. But
    the observation is a loss at 75, not "no loss".
- **Cost.** 100 spent **1.31×** the completion tokens (205,911 → 269,397) and 1.32× the
  wall time.
  - Per item, 100 spent more on 14 of 21 items. The ratio's geometric mean is 1.22×, its
    median 1.13×, its range 0.75–3.92× (m13 ran 2.1k → 8.4k).
  - Decode speed is unchanged. This is well under the paper's "1.6–1.8× longer agent
    trajectories" at max.

## What it decides

**OPP `v41-effort-passthrough-and-policy` part (2), teacher/review max → high: NOT
cleared.** The gate was "no loss at 75". This set shows a small loss at 75: two misses,
both correct at 100, statistically inconclusive and pointing the wrong way. The saving on
offer is modest, about 24% fewer tokens and a 13% median per item. So the proxy keeps
teacher/review at `max`.
- Part (1), the pass-through fixes (`xhigh`/int/`medium` coerced to max, and an explicit
  `thinking: false` flipped on), is independent of this. It stays on the grant-gated queue.
- The coding tier stays at `max` either way.
- Revisiting needs about 150+ pairs, or a set that fails more often at 100, to resolve a
  difference of about 5 points. It also needs the harness to keep the reply, so a miss can
  be classified.
